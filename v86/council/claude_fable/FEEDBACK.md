## A. GROUNDING correctness

### A.1 GSS signature criterion

**F1 — SIGN ERROR in the stated criterion (major; corrupts HC-3 as specified).**
Attacked sentence (GROUNDING §1): *"orbital stability holds when n(H_ω) — the number of negative eigenvalues of the constrained linearized energy — equals p(D), the number of positive eigenvalues of D"* with *"D_ab ≡ −∂²d/∂ω_a∂ω_b = ∂Q_a/∂ω_b"*.
Four-line check: with the doc's own convention δ(E − Σω_aQ_a) = 0, on-branch ∂d/∂ω_a = −Q_a, so d″ = −∂Q/∂ω = −D. GSS (1987/1990) states stability iff n(H_ω) = p(d″) — the positive-index of **d″**, i.e. the **negative**-index of D. Single-charge reduction with the doc's version: VK-stable branch has dQ/dω < 0 ⇒ p(D) = 0 ≠ 1 = n(H) — the stated criterion classifies the entire measured stable branch as UNSTABLE, contradicting the very sentence *"For the single-charge Q-ball this reduces to the familiar Vakhitov–Kolokolov dQ/dω < 0"*. It does not reduce; the sign is flipped.
**Fix:** criterion is n(H_ω) = n(D) (number of **negative** eigenvalues of ∂Q_a/∂ω_b), equivalently redefine D ≡ −∂Q_a/∂ω_b and keep p(D). Decoupled-limit sanity check for the 3-charge case: three independent VK-stable balls give n(D) = 3 = n(H) ✓.

**F2 — Half a criterion is provisioned: n(H_ω) is never computed (major).**
Attacked sentence (§1): *"The flavored shooter gives Q_a(ω_1,ω_2,ω_3) on the branch; finite-difference D_ab; record its signature over the partition space. The claimed 'stability region of partitions' IS the region where the GSS count matches."*
Matches against **what**? n(H_ω) — the other half of the equality — appears in no rung. It is not a constant: it is 1 in the single-charge case, 3 in the decoupled limit, and unknown on the coupled flavored branch (it can jump across the partition space, which is precisely where the physics is). HC-3 as designed produces a signature map of D with nothing to compare it to.
**Fix:** add HC-1b — constrained-Hessian negative-index on flavored backgrounds (the HC-1 BdG machinery evaluated at the flavored solution, with the charge constraints and the Gauss projector applied), at least at a handful of partitions spanning the D-signature regions. Without it, downgrade HC-3's deliverable from "stability region" to "necessary-condition scan."

**F3 — The Gauss-projection remark is hand-waved, and the cited analogy is the wrong phase (major).**
Attacked sentence (§1 caveat ii): *"gauge fields add constraint directions: the Gauss constraint must be projected before counting n(H_ω) (standard for gauged GSS, e.g. abelian Higgs vortex literature)"*.
(a) The abelian-Higgs vortex literature is the **Higgs phase**: screened, massive photon, finite-energy fluctuations. This system is the **Coulomb phase**: massless A, unscreened 1/r tails (the program's own measured London cap — screening < 1% of the flux quantum — says so). In the Coulomb phase the linearization's essential spectrum extends down to ω = 0 through the gauge channel and overlaps the soliton's zero modes — the GSS spectral hypotheses (gap between discrete negative directions and the continuum) fail as stated. I know of no theorem covering orbital stability of Coulomb-phase gauged Q-balls, and the gauged-Q-ball literature reports cases where the naive VK sign is not decisive: **CANNOT VERIFY** that any citable theorem underwrites the gauged application. (b) "Projected" is never defined: on this lattice the constraint is the discrete per-site Gauss residual (scp_sim.c:2170–2176); the projector (which fluctuation subspace, what happens to the longitudinal-A and E directions, how the compact link variables enter) determines n(H) and must be written down once, concretely, before HC-1b exists.
**Fix:** state the honest scope — GSS is a theorem for the **ungauged** three-charge problem (where the three U(1)s are exact and the spectrum is gapped) and a **heuristic** for g = 0.05, empirically supported by kernel drops; compute D and n(H) ungauged where the theorem applies; add per-partition kernel-drop verification for the gauged claims; define the lattice Gauss projector explicitly in HC-1b.

### A.2 Golden rule and channels

**F4 — The massless gauge channel makes frequency arithmetic irrelevant for every l ≥ 1 mode; the census taxonomy must fork on multipole first (major, constructive).**
Attacked sentence (§2 condition 2): *"A mode combination below the matter gap may still radiate through A if it carries a time-varying multipole — EXCEPT monopole breathing (no dipole; v85's measured protection)."*
Correct but drastically under-drawn, in two ways. (i) The gauge continuum's edge is ω = 0: for any internal mode with l ≥ 1 charge structure, **some** radiation channel is open at **any** frequency — the "gap arithmetic" (n·ω_int vs m = 1.5) decides nothing for those modes; only rates (multipole factors ~ ω^(2l+1)) and the box-IR cutoff (F6) do. The census therefore splits cleanly: l = 0 modes → gauge-forbidden, matter arithmetic governs; l ≥ 1 modes → always gauge-open, rate-suppressed, never arithmetic-protected. PROPOSAL I.1.2's *"The census of long-lived harmonics is gap arithmetic, not dynamics"* is false for the entire l ≥ 1 sector. (ii) "no dipole" mis-states the monopole protection: a spherically symmetric charge oscillation has **no time-varying exterior multipole of any order** (total charge is conserved; by the Gauss/Birkhoff argument the exterior field is static) — that is why X10c's breather is protected, and the same statement shows the protection is exactly l = 0, nothing weaker.

**F5 — Band-top formula VERIFIED; the artifact warning is incomplete on three counts.**
The formula *"ω_max² = m² + 4d/dx² (d=3)"* is correct for the 7-point Laplacian (corner of the Brillouin zone). Computed: band = [1.5, 7.54] at the CPU census resolution (N=64, L=15, dx=0.469); [1.5, 12.19] at GPU dx=0.287. Missing from the warning:
(a) **Anisotropy:** the single number ω_max is the corner top; the axis top is √(m²+4/dx²) = 4.52 (CPU) / 7.14 (GPU). Between axis top and corner top the band covers only part of the angular directions — emission is progressively phase-space-blocked and anisotropic. The artifact flag must use the **axis top as its onset**, not the corner top, or HC-2 will under-flag by a factor ~1.7 in frequency.
(b) **Lattice DOS:** near band edges the group velocity vanishes (van Hove) — rates just below an edge are enhanced, just above the gap suppressed as (nω−m)^1/2; "∝ density-of-states" must mean the lattice DOS, not the continuum one, or HC-4-vs-HC-2 comparisons will be systematically off near edges.
(c) **The θ band** [1.6, √(m_θ²+12/dx²)] is a second matter channel. At η=0 it couples to Φ modes only through the gauge sector (both are link-charged — scp_sim.c:1153–1154, current at :1237), so it is weak, but it exists and belongs in the channel list for completeness.

**F6 — The missing mirror artifact: the box-IR gauge cutoff (major; it sits exactly where the program's lines live).**
The doc warns that the lattice re-protects **high** harmonics (band top) but not that the finite box forbids **low**-frequency gauge radiation: the lowest propagating mode is ω_IR ≈ π/L = **0.209** on the CPU census boxes (N=64, L=15) and **0.057** on the GPU boxes (L=55). Every line the census targets — *"the QRK-1 lines (0.018/0.036 family; flavored splittings 0.108/0.126)"* — lies **below** the CPU cutoff, and the 0.018/0.036 family lies below even the GPU cutoff. Their gauge-channel decay is box-forbidden; their matter-channel closure needs integer combinations of order n ≥ 84 / 42 / 14 / 12 respectively (computed) — golden-rule rates at those orders are unmeasurably small. Consequence: on any currently proposed box, these lines have **no physically accessible decay channel**, and any nonzero "width" HC-4 measures is a numerical or box artifact by construction. This warning belongs in §2 with the same prominence as the band-top warning; it also governs EX-2's frequency bins and the EX-1 dipole slosh (F13).

### A.3 EX-1 adiabaticity arithmetic

**F7 — The threshold is missing the mass factor: √(2ε₁) should be √(2ε₁/m) = α_f (checked; re-grades the proposed v-span).**
Attacked sentence (§5): *"the cloud survives only if the imparted energy per unit charge ~ ½v² ≪ ε₁ → v ≪ √(2ε₁) ≈ 0.065."*
The boost adds energy per unit charge ΔE/Q = (γ−1)ω ≈ ½ω v² with ω ≈ m = 1.5 — i.e. **½mv², not ½v²**. Correct threshold: v ≪ √(2ε₁/m) = **0.0530** — which is exactly α_f (the virial orbital speed), as any correct derivation must give. Re-grading the span: v=0.05 sits at ½mv²/ε₁ = **0.89** (not "0.6·ε₁") — at threshold, not marginal; v=0.02 gives 0.14 ✓ gentle.
**Constructive pre-registration (sudden-kick form factor, hydrogenic 1s, q = mv):** P(excite/strip) = 1 − [1+(qa₀/2)²]⁻⁴ = **13% at v=0.02, 34% at v=0.035, 55% at v=0.05**. So: yes, a ramped boost is needed at the upper end — quantitatively, v=0.05 sudden excites over half the cloud. Recommend v ∈ {0.02 sudden, 0.05 ramped over τ_r ≳ 1/ε₁ ≈ 500 t.u.}, and score EX-1 against the 13%/55% numbers — that converts the rung from qualitative to falsifiable.

**F11 — HC-4 is the rung most likely to produce a misleading result (the charge's direct question).**
Attacked sentences (GROUNDING §2): *"measured linewidths of the QRK-1 lines should scale as the square of the excitation amplitude"* and *"Deviation from even-power scaling falsifies the golden-rule reading."*
Since the targeted lines have no open decay channel in-box (F6), what HC-4 will actually measure is one of: (i) **nonlinear frequency pulling** — the anharmonic line shift is itself ∝ ε² at leading order, and in finite windows a drifting line reads as a broadened line: the confound reproduces exactly the "confirming" even-power scaling; (ii) sponge absorption of the sub-IR gauge near-field (an L- and sponge-parameter-dependent loss, not a width); (iii) window-limited Fourier width 1/T; (iv) dx-dependence — STATE §4's own admission: *"no refinement pass has confirmed that any of the new v85 phenomenology (slosh periods, burn rates, split lines) is dx-converged."* The registered falsifier ("deviation from even-power") is therefore one-sided: even-power scaling confirms nothing.
**Hardening (all required before a width is reported as Γ):** time-domain complex-frequency fits (decay envelope τ separated from shift); T-window doubling invariance; L-scan and sponge-strength variation plus one periodic-BC control; one dx refinement pair; report shift and width as separate observables; and add the F10(d) calibration line as the positive control.
