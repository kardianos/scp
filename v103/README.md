## The thesis

Start from known quantum mechanics and its numerics, and stand it on
its head.

- **The vacuum is not empty.** It is a dense, self-consistent field,
  the medium or substrate. It holds field at bay.
- **"Stuff" is not a lump on a stage.** It is a void: a place where the
  medium has let go, held open by structure.
- **Colliders do not smash things.** They pour energy into the medium,
  which relaxes by opening voids.

**Relaxed in field is not relaxed in energy.** In the Higgs hat, the
held medium sits at the bottom of the brim. Letting go climbs to the
top, which costs energy. The medium therefore pushes on any relaxed
region with a pressure, the bag constant. That pressure is "holding
field at bay". If letting go released energy, the medium would let go
everywhere.

## What known physics already says

These are mainstream results. They are not prior versions of this
programme.

- **Holes.** In a filled band, or Dirac's sea, a missing fermion is a
  particle of opposite charge. For fermions the inversion is an exact
  rewrite.
- **Mott holes.** A hole in a saturated bosonic medium is a quantum
  particle. v102's vacancy is one.
- **Meissner effect, Anderson–Higgs mechanism.** A condensate gives the
  photon a mass. Field enters only through vortices, which winding
  holds open.
- **Bag model, dual superconductor.** A hadron is a bubble held by the
  vacuum's pressure, and colour field is squeezed into tubes.
- **Electron bubbles in liquid helium.** A real cavity, whose size is
  set by the medium's pressure.
- **Amplitude ("Higgs") modes.** Measured in superconductors and at
  the superfluid–Mott transition.
- **Exact maps.**
  - In 1-D, hard-core bosons are free fermions (Tonks–Girardeau).
  - In a Haldane chain, an integer-spin medium carries spin-½ ends.
- **Coulomb phases.** In spin ice and dimer models, a local exclusion
  rule makes rule-breaking defects interact as 1/r in 3-D.
- **String nets.** The ends of condensed strings can be fermions that
  carry an emergent gauge charge.
- **Bjerknes forces.** Two bubbles pulsating in phase attract as 1/r².
- **Bell's theorem.** No local classical medium reproduces quantum
  correlations. Any unification must pass it.

## Method

- **Media began as standard lattice quantum models (c1, a1, b1).**
  Under R14 a fixed lattice is a container. Later steps count
  geometry through the medium's own bonds. Bosonic
  Hubbard-type cells, with caps and modes as each lane registers them,
  and constrained bond ("chain") models.
- **Numerics are standard.**
  - Exact diagonalization (C, LAPACK/Lanczos).
  - Exact-ensemble Monte Carlo for constrained models at their
    Rokhsar–Kivelson point. There, the quantum ground state is the
    equal-amplitude superposition of valid configurations, so its
    diagonal correlations are exactly the uniform ensemble's.
  - Condensate (Gross–Pitaevskii) numerics come later, where a lane
    needs a continuum.
