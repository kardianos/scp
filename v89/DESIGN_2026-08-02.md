## Idea 1 — cells grow, bounded by harmony rather than by a constant

**The proposal.** Do not hold cells in place. Let them expand, with rules
limiting morphology per tick and a bound on extent.

**The objection.** A fixed maximum extent is a background: a length that
persists, is never converted, and that nothing can act on. Trading a
frozen graph for a frozen ruler is not progress under constraint 2.

**The proposed escape (this thread).** Let the bound be *harmonic* —
"harmonics within, dissonance between". A cell may grow while growing
keeps its intervals with its neighbours on the comb; the bound is where
the next increment costs more dissonance than it is worth. A bound made
of intervals is dimensionless and relational, so it is not a ruler.

### The model (`harmgrow.c`)

`x_i = ln r_i` with `sum x_i = 0` (scale fixing — space redistributed, not
created). Pitch `w = 1/r`, so **the interval between two cells IS their
size ratio** and "consonant neighbours" and "commensurate sizes" become
the same statement. That identification is the whole reason the idea can
work at all.

    U = sum_{i<j} w_ij D(u_ij) + sum_i (kE/2)(x_i - x*_i)^2
    u_ij = log2(r_j/r_i)
    D(u) = min over rungs [ (u-u_k)^2/(2 sigma^2) + lambda H_k ]
    rung = p/q reduced, Tenney height H = log2(pq) <= comb_limit

`x*_i` is the size cell *i*'s own energy prefers. **Nothing in U contains
a maximum size.**

### Results

**1. The staircase is real — and it is the harmonic series.** MEASURED.
Sweeping cell 0's *preferred* log2-size continuously from 0 to 3, the
*achieved* size locks to successive rungs:

| preferred log2 | achieved ratio | rung | offset from rung |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 – 0.82 | 1.0000 | 1/1 | ~5e-4 |
| 1.06 – 1.54 | 2.0000 | 2/1 | ~3e-4 |
| 1.78 – 2.02 | 3.0002 | 3/1 | ~2e-4 |
| 2.26 – 2.50 | 4.0004 | 4/1 | ~3e-4 |
| 2.72 | 5.0008 | 5/1 | ~2e-4 |
| 2.96 | 6.0009 | 6/1 | ~2e-4 |

Size is **quantised**, the plateaux are flat, and the selected ratios are
the integer overtone series. Plateaux shorten as the ratio grows, because
rungs get denser in log space — quantisation is coarse for small cells
and fine for large ones, which is a prediction, not an input.

**2. The comb holds a cell below the size its own energy wants.**
MEASURED. At `kE=1` the preferred size reaches log2 = 3.000 (8×) while
the achieved size tops out at log2 = 2.585 — rung 6/1. The next rung is
too far to pay for. **This is a bound with no bound in the potential**,
and it is made of intervals.

**3. Quantisation is a window in the competition ratio, not a limit.**
MEASURED. Comb curvature is `1/sigma^2 = 400` at `sigma=0.05`:

| kE | rungs visited | jumps / 151 samples | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | 5 | strongly quantised |
| 10 | — | — | quantised |
| 50 | 13 | 12 | quantised |
| 200 | 22 | 150 | **washed out** |
| 400 | 28 | 150 | washed out |
| 2000 | 40 | 150 | washed out |
| 8000 | 42 | 150 | washed out |

Above `kE ~ 200` every sample jumps: no plateaux, size follows preference
continuously. Quantisation exists only while the comb dominates the
cell's own elastic preference. This is the Frenkel–Kontorova
commensurate/incommensurate structure, in size rather than position —
the same physics as the PN pinning measured in `charge_fk2.c`.

**4. Negative control.** MEASURED. Same sweep with the comb collapsed to
the unison: perfectly smooth, monotone, no steps. The staircase is the
comb's, not the constraint's and not the descent's.

### The correction that mattered

The first model used a **scale-free** drive, `-g_i ln r_i` ("energy claims
space"). With random restarts it produced a beautiful staircase
`1/1 -> 4/3 -> 3/2` — unison, fourth, fifth. **It was an artifact.**
Replacing random restarts with an exhaustive **rung-seeded** search (one
relaxation initialised at every candidate rung) showed the true global
minimum is the *outermost* rung at every drive, including the weakest:
a linear drive has no scale, so it always runs to the edge of the comb
and the rung structure never selects anything.

Two lessons, both already paid for once in the charge work:

* A rugged min-over-rungs landscape needs a search over *basins that
  exist*, not random restarts. Random restarts started near the uniform
  state and reported the local minima nearest the start as if they were
  the answer.
* The drive must carry a scale. The physical drive is the cell's own
  finite energy setting a preferred size, not an unbounded pull.

### Verdict (superseded — see "Frustration kills it" below)

On a ring the idea **works, conditionally**. Harmony does quantise size and does
bound it below the cell's own preference — with no length anywhere in the
potential. But it bounds only inside a window of the comb-vs-elastic
ratio, and the "bounds fixed somehow" of the original proposal resolves
to *two* constants, not zero: `comb_limit` (the comb's span) and the
stiffness ratio. Those are dimensionless, which is the win; they are not
nothing, which is the honest caveat.

### Frustration kills it — REJECTED FOR NOW (`harmfrust.c`)

The ring staircase has one driven cell against a uniform bath, where
satisfying the comb is trivially possible. A foam cell has degree ~7 and
must hold intervals with all of them at once. `harmfrust.c` puts the same
potential on random graphs of degree 2–12.

Two things had to be fixed before the test measured anything. The comb
term grows with degree (one `D` per edge) while a fixed `kE` does not, so
comparing degrees at fixed `kE` confounds frustration with total
coupling — at `kE=1` every degree collapses to the unison crystal
(`locked_frac` 1.000, `mean_H` 0.00, every edge on 1/1) and nothing is
learned. `kE` is scaled with degree, and **every** cell is given its own
preferred size so the uniform solution is not trivially optimal.

| degree | locked_frac | mean_H | max_domain | frac at unison |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (the ring) | 0.062 | 0.00 | 3 | 0.958 |
| 3 | 0.133 | 3.95 | 5 | 0.067 |
| 4 | 0.070 | 3.40 | 2 | 0.085 |
| 6 | 0.084 | 4.01 | 6 | 0.053 |
| 8 | 0.109 | 2.79 | 4 | 0.109 |
| 10 | 0.049 | 3.71 | 3 | 0.090 |
| 12 | 0.072 | 2.75 | 5 | 0.145 |

**Once sizes are generic, consonance is essentially never satisfied.**
About 5–13% of contacts sit on a rung, at *every* degree — including the
ring. There is no trend with degree, and none with comb span either
(`locked_frac` 0.02–0.10 and `max_domain` 2–5 across `comb_limit` 1–8).
What locking survives uses high-Tenney-height rungs (`mean_H` ~3–4),
i.e. complicated ratios, not the clean overtone series.

The hoped-for consolation prize does not appear: **there is no consonant
domain scale.** `max_domain` sits at 2–6 with no structure, so the
"particle size from combinatorics" mechanism is not there to be had.

And this retro-actively bounds the ring result. Degree 2 with generic
targets gives `locked_frac` 0.062 — the staircase was **one impurity in a
uniform medium**, not a generic property of the potential.

**Caveat, stated because it is the obvious re-opening.** The rung-seeded
search was built for the one-impurity case: it seeds cell 0 at each rung
and everything else at zero. For fully heterogeneous targets that is a
poor initialisation and this is a spin-glass-like landscape, so the
measured `locked_frac` is a *lower* bound on what a better solver might
find. What is not search-limited is the **absence of any trend** with
degree or with comb span — that pattern would have to survive any solver.

**Verdict: REJECT FOR NOW.** Consonance is not a viable morphology bound
on a realistic contact graph. Re-opening requires a genuinely better
solver (annealing or belief propagation, not seeded descent) *and* a
reason to expect generic sizes to be commensurate, which nothing here
supplies.

## Idea 2b — per-cell tick counters instead of a global clock

**The proposal.** Amortise the global clock: every cell keeps its own
tick counter, "maybe a byte with a mod", advancing at its own rate.

**Why it is well-motivated.** v89 already has a local rate — the pitch law
`x = (Em + fl*load)/cap` flattens a loaded cell's pitch, and pitch *is*
rate. The physics is already local-rate; only the integrator is global.
Making the tick local formalises what the pitch law already does, and
makes time dilation structural instead of an effect computed on top of a
universal clock.

### The model (`localclock.c`)

Ring of 24 coupled phase cells. Each owns a local time and a local
**step**, so after equal elapsed coordinate time they have done different
numbers of ticks — the counters genuinely diverge. Compared against the
same ring on one global dt.

### Results

**1. The lock survives locality.** MEASURED. Order parameter
R = 0.9828 under bounded skew vs 0.9962 synchronous; with the skew bound
released, R = 0.99604, a difference of **1.1e-4**. The lock is the
physics, and a global clock was not carrying it.

**2. Bounding skew by TICK COUNT is the wrong bound.** MEASURED, and it
is the opposite of what I expected. Unbounded skew reproduced the
synchronous lock to 1e-4; *bounded* skew only to 1.3e-2. Because cells
take different-sized steps, equal tick counts are **not** equal local
time, so a tick-count bound actively distorts the ordering it is meant to
protect. **Bound skew by local time, not by tick count.**

**3. "Is a byte enough?" — yes, up to skew 127, and the failure mode is
silent.** MEASURED. The rule `K < M/2` is confirmed exactly:

| counter width M | skew K | K < M/2 | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 2 | yes | bound holds (spread 30) |
| 8 | 6 | no | **bound silently disabled** (spread 1065) |
| 16 | 4 | yes | holds (spread 46) |
| 16 | 12 | no | silently disabled |
| 64 | 40 | no | silently disabled |
| 256 | 64 | yes | holds (spread 303) |
| 256 | 200 | no | silently disabled |

The important part is *silently*. Wrap does not crash and does not
produce garbage — the wrapped comparison simply stops being able to tell
ahead from behind, so the skew test always passes and the safety property
evaporates with no symptom. A byte works, but only with the `K < 128`
invariant asserted, not assumed.

**4. Determinism is recoverable — and this is the adoptability test.**
MEASURED.

| skew K | canonical order, \|fwd−rev\| | arrival order, \|fwd−rev\| |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **0.00e+00** | 1.91e-02 |
| 2 | **0.00e+00** | 3.15e-02 |
| 4 | **0.00e+00** | 5.61e-02 |
| 8 | **0.00e+00** | 1.03e-01 |
| 16 | **0.00e+00** | 2.02e-01 |
| 64 | **0.00e+00** | 3.70e+00 |

Advancing the cell with the smallest `(t_i, i)` is **bit-identical**
regardless of scan direction. Advancing whichever eligible cell a thread
reaches first diverges, and the divergence grows with the skew allowance.

The tie-break must be **total**. An earlier version ordered by local time
alone and broke exact ties by scan position; that leaked scan order back
in at ~3e-6 — small, and *not* byte-identical. The ratchet compares
bytes, so "almost canonical" is not canonical.

### Readiness suite — READY (`localclock.c`, rewritten)

The first pass established the mechanism; the rewrite asks whether the
result can carry the battery. Degree-7 graph, N=96, energy moving on
channels as paired antisymmetric transfers.

| criterion | result |
|---|---|
| **R1 conservation** | drift **+1.4e-14** on 120.68 (1.2e-16 relative), identical to sync, and **independent of lookahead** |
| **R2 convergence** | vs a common dt/64 reference: sync falls 2.03/2.07/2.14/2.33×, async **2.05/1.94/2.03/2.07×** — first order, same solution |
| **R3 determinism** | **0.000e+00** under reversed scan, rotated origin, and both; arrival order differs by 1.2e-1 |
| **R4 parallelism** | mean **402 of 396** events eligible at once for lookahead ≥ 0.05 — effectively no barrier |

Two bugs the suite caught, both of which would have been invisible
without it:

* **Cell-driven transfers double-count.** Firing a transfer once per
  *endpoint advance* moves `gE*(h_i+h_j)` per round instead of `gE` per
  unit time — an O(1) rate error that does **not** vanish as dt→0. Making
  the **channel** own the transfer and its own clock cut the transport
  error 160× (1.8e-2 → 1.1e-4). This is the same transfer-ownership
  discipline that makes a two-pass propose/resolve conservative, and it
  is load-bearing, not stylistic.
* **A test that could not answer its own question.** Comparing async(dt)
  to sync(dt) conflates two independent O(dt) errors; both must be
  measured against a common fine reference. Under the broken form the
  async column sat at exactly 1.200e-4 for every dt, which reads
  precisely like "different fixed point" — and was actually a missing
  rescale of the channel steps in the test harness.

**The "byte with a mod" question resolves in an unexpected direction.**
Instantaneous neighbour *tick* skew reaches 1266 while local times stay
inside the lookahead, because cells with different steps diverge in tick
count without bound — that divergence *is* the dilation. So the counter
cannot be the ordering variable at any width. It does not need to be:
ordering is by local time. A byte remains fine as a cyclic phase index,
but it cannot carry causality, and the `K < M/2` rule applies only if
someone tries to make it.

### Verdict

**Ready for future testing**, with four conditions the measurements make
non-negotiable:

1. Order events by a **total function of state** (`(t_i, i)`), never by
   arrival. This is what preserves the byte-identical-rerun property the
   ratchet depends on.
2. Bound skew in **local time**, not tick count.
3. **Channels own transfers and their own clock.** Worth 160× on
   transport accuracy; without it the scheme is a different model.
4. Order by local time, not by the tick counter — the counter diverges
   without bound by construction. If a mod-M counter is used for anything
   cyclic, assert `K < M/2`: the wrap failure is silent.

It does not, by itself, remove the temporal background: it replaces one
global clock with many local ones plus a skew bound, which is a weaker
background, not none. What it does buy is that the bound is now *local*
and *physical* (it is a causality/lookahead condition, and the morphology
rate limit of idea 1 would supply exactly that lookahead), and the update
provably has no global dependency — which is the precondition for
going further.

## Where these two ideas meet

Idea 1's rate limit on morphology **is** a lookahead: a bound on how fast
a cell can affect its neighbours. Conservative event-driven execution
needs exactly such a lookahead to advance safely without global
synchronisation. So idea 1 supplies the physical quantity idea 2b needs,
and idea 2b supplies the execution model idea 1's growth contention
requires. Built in that order — canonical event ordering with
transfer-ownership conservation first, growth second — they compose.

## Idea 1b — GEOMETRIC PRESSURE as the bound (`geopress.c`)

Consonance rejected, the question became whether a **non-harmonic**
internal pressure can bound morphology. Structurally it should do better
for one reason: consonance failed by *counting* — a degree-7 cell must
satisfy seven simultaneous commensurability conditions and generically
cannot. A pressure balance is **one scalar equation per cell**, so there
is nothing to frustrate.

Model: cells in a periodic box, extent following the cell's own energy
`r = E^(1/3)` (no absolute length anywhere — only extents and
separations, both state), contact repulsion `(k/2)δ²`, and energy
conserved and flowing down `μ = (1/3)E^(-2/3)·P`. A cell under high
contact pressure sheds energy; one under low pressure absorbs. That is
the standing space-transport law arrived at from geometry rather than
imposed. **Nothing in U contains a maximum size.**

### G1 — the bound WORKS, and it is coordination-robust. MEASURED.

| box L | z | r_sd/r_mean | r_max/r_mean | dE/E |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | 3.31 | 0.045 | 1.076 | +1.1e-15 |
| 20 | 3.66 | 0.057 | 1.105 | +2.1e-16 |
| 18 | 3.76 | 0.068 | 1.158 | 0 |
| 16 | 3.89 | 0.081 | 1.177 | +2.1e-16 |
| 14 | 4.47 | 0.096 | 1.200 | 0 |
| 12 | 7.33 | 0.132 | 1.242 | +8.4e-16 |

The size distribution stays **tight and bounded at every coordination** —
no cell ever exceeds 1.24× the mean, and the spread grows only gently
with z. Conservation sits at the FP floor throughout, by construction
(paired transfers). This is exactly what consonance could not do: there
the locked fraction sat at 5–13% with no trend and no bound at all.

**Geometric pressure is a working morphology bound.**

### G2 — it does NOT localise. MEASURED, negative.

Seed a central sphere with excess energy and relax:

| boost | r_core/r_bulk at t=0 | after relax | retained |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1.257 | 1.095 | 0.371 |
| 4 | 1.583 | 1.070 | 0.119 |
| 8 | 1.995 | 0.754 | **−0.248** |
| 16 | 2.513 | 0.416 | **−0.386** |
| 32 | 3.166 | 0.614 | **−0.178** |

A hot region does not merely dissolve — it **overshoots and inverts**.
Retention goes negative: the core ends up *smaller* than the bulk. The
μ law is monotonic, so a dense core has high pressure, sheds, and keeps
shedding past uniformity.

**G3 negative control** behaves exactly as required: at `k=0`, μ ≡ 0,
nothing moves and retention is 1.000; as k rises retention collapses
(0.047 at k=0.01, −0.248 at k=1). The dispersal is the pressure's doing.

### Why G2 fails, and what it implies

This is not a tuning failure and more restarts will not fix it. A purely
repulsive contact energy is **convex**, and a convex energy has a single
minimising phase — it cannot phase-separate, so it cannot hold a
localised structure. Dispersal is the only thing it can do.

Localisation requires **non-convexity**: a μ that decreases with density
over some range, giving two coexisting phases (a van der Waals loop).
The candidate is already in the standing law table and already known to
be load-bearing — **`cap`**, the saturation law, whose probe (14/21, a
strict superset of `q_detune`) showed it is where the pitch landscape
lives. Saturation IS a non-convexity.

### Verdict

**Viable as a bound; not sufficient as a particle mechanism.** Pressure
does the job consonance could not — bounded, stable, coordination-robust
size with exact conservation and no length in the model. It does not, on
its own, hold anything together. The next question is whether adding the
`cap` saturation to the contact energy makes μ non-monotonic and opens a
coexistence region; that is a small change to `geopress.c` and is the
natural N16.

*(One artifact recorded: the first G1 run looped `q < 7` over a 6-element
array and printed a garbage `L=0.0, z=0` row. Fixed to `sizeof(Ls)/...`;
the table above is from the corrected run.)*

### N16 — `cap` saturation does NOT open coexistence. REJECTED.

The G2 diagnosis said localisation needs non-convexity, and `cap`'s own
mechanism (load flattens pitch) read mechanically gives one: a loaded
pair goes soft, `k_ij = k/(1 + (E_i+E_j)/2cap)`, which puts a negative
term in μ. Symmetric, so transfers stay paired and conservation is
untouched; `cap → ∞` recovers the unsaturated model exactly.

The `cap` sweep looked like a success — retention flips sign:

| cap | retained (boost 8) | r_max/r_mean |
|---|---|---|
| 1e12 | −0.248 (reproduces G2 exactly) | 1.997 |
| 1 | −0.256 | 2.020 |
| 0.3 | **+0.047** | 2.024 |
| 0.1 | **+0.192** | 2.017 |

**It is an artifact.** Low `cap` means soft contacts, hence slow
relaxation, so retention can be incomplete dispersal rather than a held
state. Retention vs relaxation time:

| cap | 120 | 240 | 480 | 960 | max\|f\| at 960 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1e12 | −0.248 | −0.248 | −0.248 | −0.248 | **6.2e-14** |
| 0.3 | +0.047 | −0.260 | −0.260 | −0.260 | 2.9e-04 |
| 0.1 | +0.192 | **+0.194** | −0.110 | **−0.262** | 2.3e-03 |

Every saturated case converges to the **same** dispersed, inverted state
as the unsaturated one (≈ −0.26 vs −0.248). Saturation changes only the
*rate*, not the equilibrium. There is no coexistence region.

**The methodological trap, recorded because it nearly worked.** At
cap=0.1 retention read 0.192 then 0.194 — flat across a 2× increase in
relaxation time, which is exactly what a converged plateau looks like.
It was not converged; at 4× it had gone negative and at 8× it reached
−0.262. A plateau over 2× is not convergence. What settles it is the
convergence witness, not the plateau: at 960 cycles the unsaturated run
has `max|f|` = 6.2e-14 (machine precision, genuinely converged) while
cap=0.1 is at 2.3e-3 — ten orders away. Reading the two columns together
is the whole difference between a result and an artifact.

**Why it fails, physically.** Softening the contacts lowers μ
*everywhere*, uniformly; it does not preferentially lower it in dense
regions, so it never produces the falling branch of μ(E) that coexistence
requires. It is a change of scale, not a non-convexity in the right
variable. Genuine localisation needs actual **cohesion** — an attractive
term, or a non-convexity in the size law itself — which pure contact
mechanics with any monotone stiffness cannot supply.

### Standing state of the free-cell / growth programme

* consonance as a bound — **rejected** (frustration; counting failure)
* geometric pressure as a bound — **works**, coordination-robust to
  z=7.33, `r_max/r_mean` ≤ 1.24, conservation at the FP floor
* localisation from pressure alone — **rejected** (convexity)
* localisation from `cap` saturation — **rejected** (rate, not equilibrium)

Pressure is a real morphology bound and should be adopted as one. It is
not a particle mechanism, and neither of the two candidate
non-convexities in the standing law table supplies one. The next
candidate is genuine cohesion, which is a new term rather than a
reinterpretation of an existing one — and that deserves its own
justification from PRINCIPLE before it is coded, not after.

### N17 — the contact exponent: derivation stands, experiment INVALID

**The analytic result** (worth more than any run in this section). For a
cell in a fixed cage with a general power-law contact `U = Σ(k/α)δ^α`:

    mu = (1/3) E^(-2/3) P,   P = sum_j k delta^(alpha-1)

and working out dmu/dE gives a clean criterion for the falling branch:

    mu falls with E   <=>   delta/r > (alpha - 1)/2

The non-convexity is therefore **already present in geometric pressure**
— it is simply out of reach. Harmonic contacts (α=2) need 50% overlap
against a jamming overlap of 1–10%: an order of magnitude away. Every
dispersal result in G2 and N16 is that one inequality being violated.

**This retro-actively refutes N16 on paper.** The criterion contains α
and nothing else. `cap` softening rescales `k`, so it could not move the
crossover at any value. Eight `cap` values and a four-point convergence
study established what two lines of algebra would have.

**The experiment, however, is invalid.** Two errors:

1. *Wrong density.* L=20 was inherited from G2/G4/G5 — packing fraction
   ~0.16, far below jamming (~0.64). G1's contacts came from the smaller
   boxes (L=12, z=7.33).
2. *Wrong function family, and this is the real error.* A power law with
   α<2 has force ∝ δ^(α−1) → **constant k as δ→0**: full repulsion at
   infinitesimal overlap. That is not a yielding contact, it is a hard
   shell, and it blows a dilute packing apart.

Result: `mean δ/r = 0.0000` and `max|f| = 0.0e+00` at every α — the
system escaped to a **contact-free gas**. With no contacts there is no μ
and no flow; the retention numbers are frozen transients from before
dispersal, not equilibria. The run tests nothing.

**What the correct experiment is.** A yielding contact is harmonic at
small overlap and saturates at large overlap,

    f = k*delta / (1 + delta/delta_y)

which is a different functional form, not a power law — and it has the
falling-μ property above δ_y while remaining well-behaved as δ→0. Run at
**jammed** density (L≈12, z≈7.3) where δ/r ~ 0.1 is attainable. The
prediction is that coexistence switches on as δ_y falls through the
typical overlap.

That is N18, and it is not yet run.

**Method note, recorded because it is now a pattern.** Three times in
this thread — the random-restart staircase, the ring staircase, N16's
`cap` sweep, and now this — a sweep was launched before checking that the
configuration could exhibit the effect at all. In each case the check was
cheap and analytic. The discipline that would have caught all four is the
same: *derive the criterion first, then confirm the configuration
satisfies it, then sweep.*

### THE STRUCTURAL OMISSION — a cell needs at least 3 axes

A cell here is a sphere with ONE scalar radius. That is a volume, not a
morphology. Under anisotropic load such a cell has exactly one response:
change volume, i.e. shed or absorb energy. **Dispersal was the only
channel the model had.**

With three axes (a symmetric shape tensor) a cell can

* deform at CONSTANT VOLUME — a soft mode costing no energy, absent here;
* absorb anisotropic load into shape rather than volume;
* sustain SHEAR — and with no shear resistance, no assembly can hold
  itself together against directional stress.

A particle built from scalar-radius cells has no mechanism to resist
being pulled apart. Localisation did not fail; it was made structurally
impossible and then tested in a gas.

The harmonic reading is sharper still: three axes means THREE PITCHES per
cell, so the ratios among a cell's own axes are intervals. "Harmonics
within, dissonance between" becomes a statement about a cell's INTERNAL
consonance rather than a metaphor about its neighbours — materially
different from the single `w = 1/r` given to each cell in `harmgrow.c`,
and not yet tested in any form.

   with `E` setting V and the axis ratios free — constant-volume shear

2. Contact response resolved per axis, not as a scalar overlap.
3. Run at **jammed** density (phi >= 0.64, z ~ 6-7), which is the only
   regime where holding anything together is even possible.
4. Yielding contact `f = k*delta/(1 + delta/dy)` — harmonic at small
   overlap, saturating above dy, well-behaved as delta -> 0.
5. Retention at two relaxation times, with a convergence witness, and a
   packing-fraction column printed so this cannot recur.

Only after that is a verdict on pressure-plus-shape available. Everything
above is a verdict on a sphere in a gas.

## Idea 1c — harmonics INSIDE a cell (`shapelock.c`)

Taking the 3-axis requirement seriously changes the symbolic structure.
A cell with semi-axes (a,b,c) has three internal intervals obeying an
IDENTITY,

    (a/b) * (b/c) * (c/a) = 1

which is the comb-closure condition of `charge_sym.mac` — a closed
3-cycle, with only TWO of the three intervals free.

**Why this is a different question from `harmfrust.c`.** Consonance died
between cells by counting: seven simultaneous commensurability conditions
at degree 7, generically unsatisfiable. A cell's own three axes are not a
network — one cell, one closed triangle, no neighbours to disagree with.
The mechanism that killed consonance is structurally absent here. This is
the literal reading of "harmonics within, dissonance between".

### S1 — the shape spectrum is real. EXACT (integer enumeration).

ratios at Tenney height <= ceiling. No solver, so no search artifact.

| ceiling | shapes | window-independent |

Through ceiling 3 the count is identical at both enumeration windows, so

### S2/S4 — NOT a staircase, and it is my own repeated error

Shape jumps once at t=0.45 to 16:1 — the OUTERMOST rung at ceiling 4 —
then creeps (16.003 -> 16.019). S4 confirms at every ceiling: a:b lands

### S3 — the negative control is BROKEN and tests nothing

`build_rungs(-1.0)` yields `NRUNG = 0`, not the unison: the

In `harmgrow.c` the same call accidentally worked: `RUNGS` is static and
zero-initialised, so `RUNGS[0]` IS the unison, and only the GRADIENT was
consulted there. A control that passes by accident is worse than no
control, and it means `harmgrow`'s §3 control should be re-run with an
explicit unison rung before its staircase is trusted either.

### What this establishes

Harmonics work INSIDE a cell exactly where they failed BETWEEN cells.
The difference is structural and was predictable: between cells,
d simultaneous commensurability conditions are generically unsatisfiable
(the counting failure of `harmfrust.c`); within a cell the three axes
form ONE closed triangle with two free intervals and no neighbours to
disagree with.

So "harmonics within, dissonance between" is not a slogan — it is the
correct division of labour, and it is now measured on both sides:

* **between cells**: consonance rejected (5–13% locked at every degree);
  geometric pressure bounds size instead;
* **within a cell**: consonance gives a finite shape spectrum (1, 3, 8,
  26 through ceiling 3, exactly enumerated), a shape staircase locked to
  the integer series, and an aspect-ratio bound set by the comb span.

The quantised object this growth thread was looking for is a SHAPE, not
a size.

### Harmonic demixing as a boundary mechanism — REJECTED ON COUNTING

Proposal: if overlap cost depends on the harmonic relationship between
two cells (`U ∝ D(w_i/w_j) × V_overlap`), consonant pairs share space
cheaply and dissonant pairs do not. That is differential repulsion, not
imported cohesion, and it is the Flory–Huggins/Ising demixing mechanism:
with ε_AA ≈ ε_BB ≈ 0 and ε_AB = D_max > 0 the mixing energy is 2D_max > 0,
so at zero temperature it demixes by construction, giving an interfacial
tension γ ~ D_max × (interface contact density) — a firm boundary with
width set by the overlap, no imposed length.

**The physics is right and it does not matter, for two independent
reasons, both computable from data already in hand.**

**1. Percolation.** A domain requires the consonant-bond probability to
exceed the bond percolation threshold, p_c ≈ 1/(z−1) ≈ **0.167** at z=7.
`harmfrust` measured the SCALAR lock fraction at **0.05–0.13** — already
at or below threshold, so even one-pitch consonance does not percolate.
Requiring three axes to match simultaneously gives ~p³ ~ **1e-3**, two
orders below. A cell then has ~0.007 consonant neighbours: no connected
domain exists, so there is nothing for a boundary to enclose. The
demixing mechanism has no second phase to form.

**2. Signal-to-noise.** The harmonic term modulates contact stiffness, so
its scale is β·D·ε_contact, while the foam's own geometric disorder is
σ_d = 3% annealed and 18–19% frozen — comparable or larger variation in
contact energies from geometry alone. The harmonic preference sits
underneath the disorder it would have to organise against.

### The dimensional counting that unifies all three results

| | constraint structure | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **within** a cell | ONE closed triangle, 2 free intervals | satisfiable — 26 shapes at ceiling 3 |
| **between** cells, size | d simultaneous scalar conditions | 5–13%, sub-percolation |
| **between** cells, 3-axis | 3×3 relations across d neighbours, ~p^(3d) | ~1e-3 |

**Harmonics are a per-cell structure, full stop.** They cannot mediate
between cells in 3D — not as a size bound (`harmfrust`), not as a
boundary (this). The property that makes `shapelock` work — a single
closed triangle with no environment assumed — is exactly what every
inter-cell version lacks, and going to three axes makes the inter-cell
case WORSE, not better, because it cubes the matching requirement.

Method note: the percolation check is one line against numbers already in
`harmfrust`'s log and would have taken thirty seconds. It was not done
before the mechanism was proposed. Sixth instance in this thread of
proposing before checking whether the configuration can support the
effect.

### What is left for boundaries

Not harmonics (counting), not uniform pressure (convexity). The
remaining candidates are structural rather than harmonic — contact
topology, or v89's own gated-transport machinery, which is where the
dense sector's actual physics lives and which none of this thread has
touched. Any future boundary proposal should state its percolation
fraction and its signal-to-noise against σ_d BEFORE it is coded.
