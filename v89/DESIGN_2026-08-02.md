# Design thread 2026-08-02 — growth-with-bounds, and the local clock

Two independent proposals, each taken to a concrete standalone experiment.
Neither touches `cellfab.c`. Artifacts: `harmgrow.c`, `localclock.c`;
logs `charge/runs/harmgrow.log`, `charge/runs/localclock.log`.

Status words as in `KIMI_LOG_2026-08-02.md`: **MEASURED** = numeric,
systematics named, convergence witness printed, negative control run.

---

## Idea 1 — cells grow, bounded by harmony rather than by a constant

**The proposal.** Do not hold cells in place. Let them expand, with rules
limiting morphology per tick and a bound on extent.

**The objection.** A fixed maximum extent is a background: a length that
persists, is never converted, and that nothing can act on. Trading a
frozen graph for a frozen ruler is not progress under constraint 2.

**The proposed escape (this thread).** Let the bound be *harmonic* —
"harmonics within, dissonance between". A cell may grow while growing
keeps its intervals with its neighbours on the comb; the bound is where
the next increment costs more dissonance than it is worth. A bound made
of intervals is dimensionless and relational, so it is not a ruler.

### The model (`harmgrow.c`)

`x_i = ln r_i` with `sum x_i = 0` (scale fixing — space redistributed, not
created). Pitch `w = 1/r`, so **the interval between two cells IS their
size ratio** and "consonant neighbours" and "commensurate sizes" become
the same statement. That identification is the whole reason the idea can
work at all.

    U = sum_{i<j} w_ij D(u_ij) + sum_i (kE/2)(x_i - x*_i)^2
    u_ij = log2(r_j/r_i)
    D(u) = min over rungs [ (u-u_k)^2/(2 sigma^2) + lambda H_k ]
    rung = p/q reduced, Tenney height H = log2(pq) <= comb_limit

`x*_i` is the size cell *i*'s own energy prefers. **Nothing in U contains
a maximum size.**

### Results

**1. The staircase is real — and it is the harmonic series.** MEASURED.
Sweeping cell 0's *preferred* log2-size continuously from 0 to 3, the
*achieved* size locks to successive rungs:

| preferred log2 | achieved ratio | rung | offset from rung |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 – 0.82 | 1.0000 | 1/1 | ~5e-4 |
| 1.06 – 1.54 | 2.0000 | 2/1 | ~3e-4 |
| 1.78 – 2.02 | 3.0002 | 3/1 | ~2e-4 |
| 2.26 – 2.50 | 4.0004 | 4/1 | ~3e-4 |
| 2.72 | 5.0008 | 5/1 | ~2e-4 |
| 2.96 | 6.0009 | 6/1 | ~2e-4 |

Size is **quantised**, the plateaux are flat, and the selected ratios are
the integer overtone series. Plateaux shorten as the ratio grows, because
rungs get denser in log space — quantisation is coarse for small cells
and fine for large ones, which is a prediction, not an input.

**2. The comb holds a cell below the size its own energy wants.**
MEASURED. At `kE=1` the preferred size reaches log2 = 3.000 (8×) while
the achieved size tops out at log2 = 2.585 — rung 6/1. The next rung is
too far to pay for. **This is a bound with no bound in the potential**,
and it is made of intervals.

**3. Quantisation is a window in the competition ratio, not a limit.**
MEASURED. Comb curvature is `1/sigma^2 = 400` at `sigma=0.05`:

| kE | rungs visited | jumps / 151 samples | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | 5 | strongly quantised |
| 10 | — | — | quantised |
| 50 | 13 | 12 | quantised |
| 200 | 22 | 150 | **washed out** |
| 400 | 28 | 150 | washed out |
| 2000 | 40 | 150 | washed out |
| 8000 | 42 | 150 | washed out |

Above `kE ~ 200` every sample jumps: no plateaux, size follows preference
continuously. Quantisation exists only while the comb dominates the
cell's own elastic preference. This is the Frenkel–Kontorova
commensurate/incommensurate structure, in size rather than position —
the same physics as the PN pinning measured in `charge_fk2.c`.

**4. Negative control.** MEASURED. Same sweep with the comb collapsed to
the unison: perfectly smooth, monotone, no steps. The staircase is the
comb's, not the constraint's and not the descent's.

### The correction that mattered

The first model used a **scale-free** drive, `-g_i ln r_i` ("energy claims
space"). With random restarts it produced a beautiful staircase
`1/1 -> 4/3 -> 3/2` — unison, fourth, fifth. **It was an artifact.**
Replacing random restarts with an exhaustive **rung-seeded** search (one
relaxation initialised at every candidate rung) showed the true global
minimum is the *outermost* rung at every drive, including the weakest:
a linear drive has no scale, so it always runs to the edge of the comb
and the rung structure never selects anything.

Two lessons, both already paid for once in the charge work:

* A rugged min-over-rungs landscape needs a search over *basins that
  exist*, not random restarts. Random restarts started near the uniform
  state and reported the local minima nearest the start as if they were
  the answer.
* The drive must carry a scale. The physical drive is the cell's own
  finite energy setting a preferred size, not an unbounded pull.

### Verdict

The idea **works, conditionally**. Harmony does quantise size and does
bound it below the cell's own preference — with no length anywhere in the
potential. But it bounds only inside a window of the comb-vs-elastic
ratio, and the "bounds fixed somehow" of the original proposal resolves
to *two* constants, not zero: `comb_limit` (the comb's span) and the
stiffness ratio. Those are dimensionless, which is the win; they are not
nothing, which is the honest caveat.

## Idea 2b — per-cell tick counters instead of a global clock

**The proposal.** Amortise the global clock: every cell keeps its own
tick counter, "maybe a byte with a mod", advancing at its own rate.

**Why it is well-motivated.** v89 already has a local rate — the pitch law
`x = (Em + fl*load)/cap` flattens a loaded cell's pitch, and pitch *is*
rate. The physics is already local-rate; only the integrator is global.
Making the tick local formalises what the pitch law already does, and
makes time dilation structural instead of an effect computed on top of a
universal clock.

### The model (`localclock.c`)

Ring of 24 coupled phase cells. Each owns a local time and a local
**step**, so after equal elapsed coordinate time they have done different
numbers of ticks — the counters genuinely diverge. Compared against the
same ring on one global dt.

### Results

**1. The lock survives locality.** MEASURED. Order parameter
R = 0.9828 under bounded skew vs 0.9962 synchronous; with the skew bound
released, R = 0.99604, a difference of **1.1e-4**. The lock is the
physics, and a global clock was not carrying it.

**2. Bounding skew by TICK COUNT is the wrong bound.** MEASURED, and it
is the opposite of what I expected. Unbounded skew reproduced the
synchronous lock to 1e-4; *bounded* skew only to 1.3e-2. Because cells
take different-sized steps, equal tick counts are **not** equal local
time, so a tick-count bound actively distorts the ordering it is meant to
protect. **Bound skew by local time, not by tick count.**

**3. "Is a byte enough?" — yes, up to skew 127, and the failure mode is
silent.** MEASURED. The rule `K < M/2` is confirmed exactly:

| counter width M | skew K | K < M/2 | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 2 | yes | bound holds (spread 30) |
| 8 | 6 | no | **bound silently disabled** (spread 1065) |
| 16 | 4 | yes | holds (spread 46) |
| 16 | 12 | no | silently disabled |
| 64 | 40 | no | silently disabled |
| 256 | 64 | yes | holds (spread 303) |
| 256 | 200 | no | silently disabled |

The important part is *silently*. Wrap does not crash and does not
produce garbage — the wrapped comparison simply stops being able to tell
ahead from behind, so the skew test always passes and the safety property
evaporates with no symptom. A byte works, but only with the `K < 128`
invariant asserted, not assumed.

**4. Determinism is recoverable — and this is the adoptability test.**
MEASURED.

| skew K | canonical order, \|fwd−rev\| | arrival order, \|fwd−rev\| |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **0.00e+00** | 1.91e-02 |
| 2 | **0.00e+00** | 3.15e-02 |
| 4 | **0.00e+00** | 5.61e-02 |
| 8 | **0.00e+00** | 1.03e-01 |
| 16 | **0.00e+00** | 2.02e-01 |
| 64 | **0.00e+00** | 3.70e+00 |

Advancing the cell with the smallest `(t_i, i)` is **bit-identical**
regardless of scan direction. Advancing whichever eligible cell a thread
reaches first diverges, and the divergence grows with the skew allowance.

The tie-break must be **total**. An earlier version ordered by local time
alone and broke exact ties by scan position; that leaked scan order back
in at ~3e-6 — small, and *not* byte-identical. The ratchet compares
bytes, so "almost canonical" is not canonical.

### Verdict

Adoptable, with three conditions the measurements make non-negotiable:

1. Order events by a **total function of state** (`(t_i, i)`), never by
   arrival. This is what preserves the byte-identical-rerun property the
   ratchet depends on.
2. Bound skew in **local time**, not tick count.
3. A byte is enough for the counter, but assert `K < M/2` — the wrap
   failure is silent.

It does not, by itself, remove the temporal background: it replaces one
global clock with many local ones plus a skew bound, which is a weaker
background, not none. What it does buy is that the bound is now *local*
and *physical* (it is a causality/lookahead condition, and the morphology
rate limit of idea 1 would supply exactly that lookahead), and the update
provably has no global dependency — which is the precondition for
going further.

## Where these two ideas meet

Idea 1's rate limit on morphology **is** a lookahead: a bound on how fast
a cell can affect its neighbours. Conservative event-driven execution
needs exactly such a lookahead to advance safely without global
synchronisation. So idea 1 supplies the physical quantity idea 2b needs,
and idea 2b supplies the execution model idea 1's growth contention
requires. Built in that order — canonical event ordering with
transfer-ownership conservation first, growth second — they compose.
